\chapter*{Abstract}

%An abstract of not more than 300 words must be included and will indicate the major emphasis of the thesis, new discoveries, and its contribution to knowledge.
Product Line Engineering (PLE) has become a common engineering approach for managing product complexity and reuse across multiple industries. The practice is used to identify reusable code, compose software components, and model product variation. There is, however, a gap in the generation of system traceability when using PLE. Specifically, it is challenging to trace through a feature model from requirements to design elements. Further, traceability itself is both costly and tedious, often completed at the end of development to meet certification standards or for assurance purposes. However, traceability is crucial for supporting change-impact analysis during both the development and maintenance of a product. This thesis proposes a methodology to improve feature identification using \gls{MDE} techniques, define a hierarchical relationship between features and requirements, and provide a tool-supported approach to generating and maintaining traceability in a PLE context throughout a product life-cycle. The contributions of this thesis address the gap between \gls{PLE} and requirement engineering to migrate traceability from a retrospective task to a more active part of development.
